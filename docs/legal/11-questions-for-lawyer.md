# Questions for the lawyer: children's data and medical-device rules (5.11, 5.12) ⚖️

## Children's data and the healthcare exemption (5.11)

The DPDP Act s. 9 requires verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian before processing a child's data, and forbids tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising aimed at children. The DPDP Rules (Fourth Schedule) exempt certain classes of Data Fiduciaries and purposes, including clinical establishments and healthcare professionals, from parts of these duties **to the extent necessary** to protect the child's health.

What TJS does now: always asks a parent or guardian for a child, records who and their relationship, and asks again when the child turns 18 or the guardian changes.

Questions:
1. Does the exemption cover a private speech therapy clinic, a hospital department and a university teaching clinic alike? Does it depend on registration as a clinical establishment?
2. Which purposes does it cover: assessment and therapy only, or also records, reminders, student involvement and teaching? (Our reading: only what is *necessary* for health; we ask for the rest anyway.)
3. What counts as "verifiable" in a clinic? TJS records the parent's own signature (or signed form / thumbprint with a witness) as the consent, and separately an identity check: known to the clinic, ID seen (type only), or a one-time code to their registered phone. Is that enough, and should the identity check be mandatory for children?
4. For 16–17-year-olds, should we also ask the young person themselves?
5. Retention for children's records: until when after they turn 18?

## Staying outside medical-device rules (5.12)

Under the Medical Devices Rules 2017 (CDSCO), software can be a medical device if it is intended for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of a disease or disability.

**TJS's position:** a planning, scheduling, documentation and consent tool. It records what clinicians decide. It does **not** diagnose, score or interpret tests automatically, suggest diagnoses or severity, or recommend treatment. Accuracy percentages are arithmetic on the clinician's own trial counts; "trend" labels describe the clinician's recorded numbers.

Rules we follow so it stays that way: no automated diagnosis; no treatment recommendations; no risk scores; any future AI feature (e.g. writing help) stays assistive, shown as a suggestion the clinician must review, and never makes clinical decisions.

Questions:
1. Do you agree TJS is not a medical device under the 2017 Rules and CDSCO's notifications on software?
2. Would features such as automated progress graphs, goal templates or AI-drafted report text change that?
3. If we serve EU clinics later: is it outside the EU MDR (Rule 11)?
